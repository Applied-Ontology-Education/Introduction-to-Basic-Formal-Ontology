# Syllabus

This public learning path follows the Fall 2026 course outline. Consult the [complete course page](https://aowiki.nsm.buffalo.edu/index.php/Introduction_to_Basic_Formal_Ontology:_Fall_2026) for authoritative details and updates.

## Course description

Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) is a top-level, ISO-standard ontology framework designed to provide a shared, reusable foundation for domain ontologies. The course examines BFO's central distinctions, realist foundations, formal axiomatization in OWL and first-order logic, and use in cross-domain knowledge integration.

## Weekly learning path

### Week 1 — Orientation and background

Establish the motivations for top-level ontologies, shared semantics, and BFO.

### Week 2 — Basic Formal Ontology 101

[Watch the lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sbzF9p7qvk). Focus on building ontologies with BFO and deciding whether a general term designates a universal.

### Week 3 — BFO tutorial series

[Watch the six-part BFO Tutorial (2019)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLyngZgIl3WTj6tWcypTLpCnYXu6o93kD4). This series surveys BFO 2.0; the core ontology remains applicable, while BFO 2020 adds the formalization associated with the ISO standard.

### Week 4 — Realism and ontology engineering

[Watch the Basic Formal Ontology Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GWkk5AfRCpM). Consider how a realist ontology represents designs and other information artifacts that may be about entities that do not yet exist.

### Week 5 — The ontology of science

[Watch the lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PwsBxRs9kns). Examine biological organization across levels of granularity, from molecules and cells to organisms and biological processes.

### Week 6 — Information artifacts, aboutness, and language

Watch [Information Artifacts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sTx_rRWmTqE), [Aboutness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PBKsupBquok), and [Language](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y3btP1InPZY). Relate generically dependent continuants to information artifacts and aboutness.

### Week 7 — Ontology of terrorism

[Watch the lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G4lg1_-XpiE). Explore an ontological analysis of terrorism that draws on speech acts, language capabilities, behavior, violence, and threats.

### Week 8 — Capabilities

[Watch Capabilities (2022)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jH1sc7FTs3w). Compare capabilities with functions and dispositions, and examine proposed axioms and cross-domain examples.

### Week 9 — BFO and DOLCE

[Watch the lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XTVR7k63_VA). Compare approaches to top-level ontology and consider how BFO supports its users.

### Week 10 — Relatively isolated systems

[Watch the lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OUr0tuFloM). Study the influence of Roman Ingarden's ontology and the treatment of systems of different types.

### Week 11 — ISO/IEC 21838

[Watch the lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_0masZPGLb0) and review the [ISO/IEC 21838-2 standard page](https://www.iso.org/standard/74572.html). Consider BFO as a standardized top-level ontology supporting information interchange.

### Week 12 — Tutorials and temporalized relations

Watch [JOWO Tutorial, Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYDe09TOw2M), [JOWO Tutorial, Part 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wh_KZGXc1Es), and [BFO 2020 Temporalized Relations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fkkWkTIxrNQ). Examine strategies for representing time-indexed relations in OWL.

### Week 13 — The Emotion Ontology

[Watch the lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18php_34s-M). Consider a BFO-aligned treatment of emotion.

### Weeks 14–15 — Learner presentations

Present and discuss analyses of BFO's use, reception, or application. Course-specific presentation dates and submission instructions are maintained on the complete course page.
